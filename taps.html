The research question

What survey inputs are useful for classifying grief-journey stages, and can a shorter analytical process support staff review?

Data & methodology

Structured Likert-scale questions and English/Spanish open-text responses support classification into six grief-journey stages. Survey records are not included in the public repository.

  1. Compared structured predictive models, text-mining approaches and variable-selection approaches.
  2. Used caret for q4–q20 survey predictors, range preprocessing, leave-one-out cross-validation and Accuracy selection.
  3. The project poster identifies Random Forest with range and YeoJohnson preprocessing as its leading model.
  4. Explored language detection, optional translation, tokenization and word frequencies in a separate text-analysis branch.

What the evidence shows

6Grief-journey stages
0.889Poster-reported Kaggle score

The project poster reports Random Forest as the best-performing model, with a Kaggle score of 0.889. Selected structured models outperform the displayed text-mining and variable-selection variants.

Implications

The project explored how survey classification could support staff review and whether fewer inputs could preserve classification quality. Process maps described prospective workflow benefits.
